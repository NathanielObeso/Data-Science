The dataset SoCalRent2025 features three counties: Los Angeles (LA), Orange (OC), and Riverside (RIV). The distribution is fairly even, with each county having 100 houses apiece. We shall split it into three different datasets:

SoCalRent2025_LAC <- SoCalRent2025 %>% 
  filter(county == "LA")

SoCalRent2025_OC <- SoCalRent2025 %>% 
  filter(county == "OC")

SoCalRent2025_RIV <- SoCalRent2025 %>% 
  filter(county == "RIV")

As I was doing my exploratory data analysis, I realized that SoCalRent2025 has commas and dashes in some of the observations of square feet. The variable sq ft is a character variable, so I had to make it numeric, but I had to change some things. First, I removed the commas from the numbers in @import-relevant-datasets to make them ready to be converted to a numeric variable.

Another thing that stood out was the mean price of LA County and Orange County are both much higher than that of Riverside, and I mainly wanted to compare those two counties, so I want to see if the housing prices are significantly different, and potentially why they are if that’s the case.

Right off the bat, a rent for $30000 in OC, a house in Anaheim that has 2 bedrooms and 2 bathrooms. It doesn’t sound like it is the norm, so we will remove this outlier. We can also filter out this $20000 rent in LA county, though that one has 5 bedrooms and 4 bathrooms, among the highest amount of either within the entire dataset, so I am content with keeping it there for now.

SoCalRent2025_minimums <- SoCalRent2025 %>%
  filter(!is.na(`sq ft`)) %>% 
  mutate(
    `sq ft` = as.numeric(str_extract(`sq ft`, "[0-9]+"))
  )

SoCalRent2025_maximums <- SoCalRent2025 %>% 
  filter(!is.na(`sq ft`)) %>% 
  mutate(
    `sq ft` = as.numeric(if_else(str_detect(`sq ft`, "-"),
      true = str_remove(str_extract(`sq ft`, "-[0-9]+$"), "-"),
      false = str_extract(`sq ft`, "[0-9]+"))
      )
  )

When we remove it, the average rent price in orange county is now $4557.73, cutting the difference between the mean OC rent price with the outlier and the mean LA rent price, but it’s still pretty high.

It is worth noting that the only things that are worth more than $10,000 are houses, which makes sense because houses tend to have more square feet. Speaking of square feet, the same outlier also has the highest square feet of any home in the entire dataset! Nearly 4,000 more square feet than the third quartile! Whereas there are still outliers for prices of a house, however, the highest value is only just above 3,500 square feet (3,614 to be exact), which is another house that costs over $10,000 as the house itself costs $15,500.
